# Meta Behavioral Interview Stories - Staff System Software Engineer

## Category: Managing Priorities & Ambiguity

### 1. Managing Competing Priorities to Deliver a Project
**Situation:** At Adobe, I was leading the DPV Code Coverage initiative while simultaneously supporting a critical customer escalation for a chip verification issue.
**Task:** I needed to deliver both the new coverage tool and resolve the customer issue, each with tight deadlines and high visibility.
**Action:** I prioritized by impact, delegated routine tasks, and set up daily syncs with both teams. I communicated trade-offs transparently to stakeholders and negotiated deadline extensions where possible.
**Result:** Both projects were delivered successfully. The coverage tool prevented costly redesigns, and the customer escalation was resolved ahead of schedule.
**Reflection:** Learned the importance of ruthless prioritization, clear communication, and leveraging team strengths.

### 2. Working on a Project with Little or No Direction
**Situation:** At Microsoft, I was tasked with integrating Stream 2.0 video APIs into PowerPoint Recording Studio, but requirements were vague and documentation was sparse.
**Task:** Deliver a seamless video integration despite ambiguity.
**Action:** I proactively reached out to API owners, reverse-engineered sample apps, and set up a cross-team working group to clarify requirements.
**Result:** Delivered a stable integration that improved user experience and was later reused across other Office products.
**Reflection:** Learned to thrive in ambiguity by seeking information, building alliances, and iterating quickly.

### 3. Acting Quickly Without a Clear Path
**Situation:** During a critical production incident in OneDrive Catalog SKU Filtering, unsupported SKUs were exposed, causing customer outages.
**Task:** Act fast to mitigate impact without a clear root cause.
**Action:** I led a war room, coordinated with engineering and marketing, and implemented a temporary SKU filter while root cause analysis was ongoing.
**Result:** Outage was contained within hours, and a permanent fix was rolled out in days.
**Reflection:** Learned to balance speed and caution, and the value of decisive leadership under pressure.

### 4. Pivoting Mid-Project Due to Change
**Situation:** In the RIBS to Hyperdrive migration at Synopsys, we hit major roadblocks with the initial approach.
**Task:** Deliver migration despite unexpected technical challenges.
**Action:** I proposed a hybrid migration strategy, coordinated with build systems, and reallocated resources to unblock progress.
**Result:** Migration completed ahead of schedule, reducing installation time by 50%.
**Reflection:** Learned to embrace change, pivot quickly, and communicate rationale to stakeholders.

### 5. Project Grew in Scope/Timeline Unexpectedly
**Situation:** Save/Restore Design Enhancement at Adobe started as a minor feature but grew into a major overhaul due to customer feedback.
**Task:** Deliver expanded scope without derailing other commitments.
**Action:** I renegotiated timelines, added phased delivery, and kept stakeholders informed.
**Result:** Feature was delivered in stages, improving customer workflows and satisfaction.
**Reflection:** Learned to manage scope creep and maintain delivery discipline.

## Category: Conflict, Feedback & Relationships

### 6. Disagreement with Colleague, Realizing Initial Stance Was Wrong
**Situation:** During Token-based Licensing project, I initially pushed for a fully shared pool, but a colleague argued for dedicated minimums.
**Task:** Resolve disagreement and deliver best solution.
**Action:** I reviewed usage data, listened to concerns, and realized my colleague’s approach better balanced team needs.
**Result:** We implemented a hybrid system, improving license utilization and team buy-in.
**Reflection:** Learned humility, value of diverse perspectives, and importance of data-driven decisions.

### 7. Most Difficult Working Relationship
**Situation:** At Microsoft, I worked with a partner PM who frequently changed requirements late in the cycle.
**Task:** Deliver project despite friction.
**Action:** I set up regular check-ins, documented decisions, and escalated only when necessary.
**Result:** Project delivered with fewer last-minute changes and improved collaboration.
**Reflection:** Learned patience, proactive communication, and boundary-setting.

### 8. Pushing for Unpopular Change
**Situation:** Advocated for architectural overhaul in Web Editor Performance at Adobe, facing resistance from legacy system owners.
**Task:** Drive change despite opposition.
**Action:** Presented performance data, ran pilot tests, and involved skeptics in design reviews.
**Result:** Achieved 60% performance improvement and enabled future roadmap.
**Reflection:** Learned to build consensus and use data to drive change.

### 9. Receiving Actionable Feedback
**Situation:** After a demo, my manager noted my technical explanations were too abstract for non-engineers.
**Task:** Improve communication.
**Action:** I sought feedback, practiced with cross-functional peers, and simplified my messaging.
**Result:** My presentations became more effective, leading to broader adoption of my projects.
**Reflection:** Learned the power of clear, audience-tailored communication.

### 10. Most Difficult Project Relationship
**Situation:** On PowerPoint Recording Studio, a designer and I disagreed on UI priorities.
**Task:** Align on user experience.
**Action:** Facilitated user testing, shared results, and compromised on design elements.
**Result:** Achieved a user-friendly interface and strong adoption.
